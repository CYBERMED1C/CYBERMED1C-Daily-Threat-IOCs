Multiple artifacts or alternate hashes of a known campaign do not inflate novel-threat counts.

Most entries have a single primary investigation. Coverage limitations: CISA advisory/RSS/KEV and selected UK NCSC routes remained inaccessible or unusable. CERT Polska index failures were partly recovered through an official individual advisory. Selected ThreatFox, URLhaus, MalwareBazaar and Hatching Triage individual records could not be inspected; listings/scores alone supplied no accepted IOCs. MyCERT remained inaccessible. Some regular-roster indexes exposed older research or no usable current report. English Ostorlab, npm registry and Bitbison registry routes failed; the official French report, two original OpenSSF records and successful public build logs were inspected. These access gaps limit coverage and corroboration; they do not establish a quiet threat day.

Both hunts use a rolling 30-day lookback and a final 500-result cap after IOC filtering. The three IPs are reviewed destinations. Defender requires DeviceNetworkEvents, DeviceFileEvents and DeviceProcessEvents. Elastic requires the listed log indexes with suitable ECS keyword/hash/URL fields and destination.ip mapped as IP. Exact hostname matching does not widen to every subdomain; exact full-URL matching preserves path case and the malicious shared-service path. Missing URL paths, encrypted DNS/QUIC visibility and absent hash fields can prevent hits.

File hashes require recorded file/process telemetry; DLLs loaded without a corresponding recorded file event can be missed. SHA-256 is often unpopulated in Defender and no alternate hashes were invented. Older documented activity may fall outside the 30-day hunt. Domains and addresses can be suspended, reassigned or change ownership; investigate matches in their documented context. Static syntax/type/set/escaping checks were performed; the hunts were not executed against live Defender or Elastic platforms. No malicious infrastructure or malware sample was contacted, downloaded or executed.
