## Coverage

Every accepted value has single-source first-party evidence. No independent corroboration or live-current activity confirmation is claimed. `NEW` means first collected to this archive. Confidence and acceptance scores are unassigned; unknown observation dates remain null. MD5 type is inferred from the appendix's 32-hexadecimal file-hash form.

CISA and CERT Polska access failed; several ThreatFox, MalwareBazaar and sandbox detail pages were unreadable. UK/Canadian and other government index checks did not yield usable same-day IOC evidence. Several vendor indexes were stale or inaccessible. These gaps and the short research duration limit coverage; a small accepted set does not imply a quiet threat day.

All five files derive from the same accepted set. Hunts use a rolling 30-day lookback and a 500-result limit after IOC filtering. Domain matching uses exact hostnames without widening to parents. The IP is matched only as a destination supported by the source's exfiltration role. File hashes require corresponding telemetry; Defender SHA256 may be missing and extension resource files may lack hash events. MD5 is a hunting identifier, not a cryptographic integrity guarantee.

Defender requires DeviceNetworkEvents, DeviceFileEvents and DeviceProcessEvents. Elastic requires suitable `logs-*` ECS mappings for `@timestamp`, `destination.ip`, `destination.domain`, `dns.question.name`, `url.domain`, and file/process hash fields. Adjust the index and mappings for your deployment; missing fields or mixed field types may cause query errors. No live Defender or Elastic execution is claimed. The lookback will not recover March–May activity unless those events fall within retained queried data and the window is deliberately adjusted.
