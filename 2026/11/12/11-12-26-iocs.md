Public threat-hunting research by CYBERMEDIC / CYBERMED1C. Report date: America/Los_Angeles. Evidence collection timestamps: UTC.

## Executive summary

114 validated indicators cover 12 campaign or malware context groups. All are newly collected to this repository after comparison with its complete 42-record structured archive. Indicator counts include related components, packages and platform builds; they are not counts of new threats, infections or newly active malware.

The strongest fresh reporting concerns SCKit deployment and WFX credential theft. The set also retains useful recently published retrospective research and explicitly dated November observations. Publication today does not establish activity today.

## Important campaigns and malware activity

- SCKit: first-party endpoint evidence documents November 11 deployment of rogue ScreenConnect clients and the toolkit. Recovered console-takeover attempts did not demonstrate successful worm propagation. Initial-access conclusions remain qualified while the vendor investigates.
- WFX: November 12 reports document credential-stealing workflows from November 10 onward. Exact collectors are retained with scoped ports; later Telegram-only variants are a coverage gap.
- Cereal: ESET published new research on November 10 concerning older Exchange compromises. Many components run in memory, so file hashes alone cannot cover the toolset.
- SleepyDuck / EtherDuck: compromised release automation delivered malicious bee-js and Swarm Desktop builds. Previously reported stage-3 files were reused; release artifacts are not separate novel threats.
- Miasma / testmetest: the Ruby gem was published November 1; a separately documented November 9 build targeted AI-agent workflows. Shared code does not prove shared operators.
- TinstaPorn and fragment-api-py: recent original reports describe an older Android sample and a November 8 malicious Python release. Shared-service configuration paths are kept exact; passive or benign helper files are excluded.
- ClickFix / DuskDesk: directly documented malicious code and a delivery domain are retained. Inactive/old broad infrastructure, ambiguous runtimes and a token-bearing download URL were excluded.
- Citrus / Sn00py: one exact delivery URL adds coverage to an already tracked campaign, using a visually reviewed publisher figure.
- Hermes / Carbonato-linked Docker attacks: new November reports document older July–September evidence. Payload-delivery/callback ports and one configured tunnel service are narrowly scoped; successful callbacks and current activity are not assumed. The model gateway, mixed registries, fallback-only routes and unresolved peers were excluded.
- FlyAgent and Agent Tesla: only one SHA256 per concrete sandbox case is included; submission time is not wild first-seen time. Embedded credentials and legitimate service endpoints were omitted.

## Coverage

Broad two-pass coverage was achieved, with material access limitations. CISA direct pages and some Sophos checks failed; a canonical joint government advisory was recovered through FBI IC3. Some abuse.ch record pages returned user-agent verification gates, which were not bypassed. SentinelOne access was uneven; available index dates did not justify importing old indicators. Other rosters/feeds without eligible recent evidence were checked as discovery only.

The Trend Micro research-site move was verified from its official domains. Current TrendAI reports and both public technical briefs were reviewed; a PDF reader cache failure was recovered from the vendor-linked public PDF. A future-dated November 16 index entry was not treated as published evidence.

Corroborating reports can share artifacts. Unless stated otherwise, an exact value is supported by one primary publisher or sandbox; multiple articles are not proof of independent field observations. No current-active claims are inferred from source availability, registration dates, sandbox submissions or publication dates.

Both hunts use a rolling 30-day window and a final 500-result cap after IOC filtering. Destination IP ports are enforced where documented. Exact hostnames and full URL paths are used; URL path/query case is preserved. Defender requires DeviceNetworkEvents, DeviceFileEvents and DeviceProcessEvents; Elastic requires the named ECS fields and suitable index mappings. Full URLs, SHA256, Android/package/configuration files and in-memory components may have limited telemetry. Neither query was executed or parsed on a live Defender or Elastic deployment.
