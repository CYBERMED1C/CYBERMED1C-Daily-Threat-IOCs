Publisher migrations were followed where verified; some regular-roster sources exposed only older research or no usable current study. Intermittent report failures were recovered through readable cached report references where available. These gaps limit corroboration and do not establish a quiet threat day.

Both hunts use a rolling 30-day lookback and a final 500-result limit after IOC filtering. The nine IPs are reviewed C2/payload destinations. Defender requires DeviceNetworkEvents, DeviceFileEvents and DeviceProcessEvents. Elastic requires the listed log indexes, populated ECS keyword/hash/URL fields, and destination.ip mapped as IP. Exact host matching does not automatically cover random subdomains; exact full-URL matching retains path case and shared-service endpoint scope. URL serialization, missing paths, encrypted traffic and absent hashes can prevent matches.

DLLs, reflectively loaded payloads and memory-only BPF objects can evade ordinary file/process hash telemetry. SHA-256 is often absent in Defender; no missing alternate hashes were invented. Older documented activity may fall outside the 30-day hunt. Domains and addresses can be suspended or reassigned; investigate matches in their source context. Static set, syntax, escaping and type checks were performed; queries were not executed against live Defender or Elastic platforms. No malicious infrastructure or malware samples were contacted, downloaded or executed.
