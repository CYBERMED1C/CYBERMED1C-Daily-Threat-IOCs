## Important campaigns and malware activity

**VeilDHT.** 26 appendix indicators distinguish actual C2 addresses from discovery domains whose DNS responses may encode spoofed addresses. Publication is November 26; the report describes October 16–26 observations.

**Remus and Vinci RAT.** Six Remus components concern malicious browser extension email interception. Vinci RAT evidence includes observed landing/C2 infrastructure and six component hashes. Signed benign installers and DNS-only IP associations are excluded. Both original studies were published November 24; this does not establish activity on the report date.

**Volant Manticore.** The November 24 report supports malicious KuboShell/Homa artifacts and selected directly analyzed infrastructure. Underlying activity spans older 2025–2026 campaigns. Dual-use RMM packages and loosely associated infrastructure are excluded. Actor attribution remains the publisher’s assessment.

**PolinRider.** StepSecurity documents poisoned pull requests and wallet advertisements continuing November 27. SafeDep independently analyzes a Python loader and the shared server wallet. Advertisements do not establish that a server was contacted or that its current payload was obtained. LF/CRLF and decoded-stage hashes are artifacts of the same operation.

**SYS010.** Bitdefender’s November 26 analysis documents malicious extension and loader infrastructure used for Meta account theft. Legitimate signed executables, public mail services and the browser-management account domain are excluded. Individual IOC observation dates are unspecified.

**SoftwareOK.** AhnLab’s November 26 investigation distinguishes malicious Q-Dir/DesktopOK installers from legitimate hashes. Only explicit malware digests, unambiguous malicious comparison cells, an exact RAT-package URL and the port-scoped RAT server are retained.

**Hugging Face phishing and MadApe.** Aikido’s November 24 research lists 42 exact tenants inspected October 16 and all paused or removed by October 29. These are retrospective indicators, with little overlap with a 30-day hunt. Group-IB’s November 25 MadApe report supplies selected analyzed phishing pages and operator panels; recipient-specific paths, shared hosting and broad pivots are excluded.

**clampony / enterotoxin and storefront fraud.** SafeDep’s November 24 report, updated November 26, documents exact stealer paths and RAT artifacts. Empty execution stubs and certificate validation can prevent execution; compromise is not assumed. A separate FortiGuard report supplies one concretely inspected fraudulent storefront.
